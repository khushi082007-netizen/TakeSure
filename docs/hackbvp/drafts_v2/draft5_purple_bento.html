1The problem

  • Two medical systems, zero cross-checking. A thyroid patient on Thyronorm adds ashwagandha; a transplant patient on tacrolimus drinks giloy kadha; a cardiac patient on warfarin takes guggul.
  • Each pair can cause harm: ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone, giloy can oppose immunosuppression, guggul can increase bleeding.
  • Nobody asks, nobody tells. The doctor rarely asks about herbs, the patient rarely mentions them, the chemist has no tool, the label says nothing.
  • Existing tools do not cover it. Medscape, Drugs.com and 1mg check only allopathic × allopathic pairs; Western herb databases are English-only, paid and do not know Indian brands like Himalaya Ashvagandha or Dabur Giloy Ghanvati.

2Proposed solution

  • Scan both. The patient photographs the allopathic prescription and the Ayurvedic product label (or types the names); every name is confirmed before checking.
  • Match Indian names. Brand → generic (Thyronorm → levothyroxine) and product → herbs (Chyawanprash → amla, ashwagandha…) with Sanskrit / Hindi / Latin synonyms.
  • Decide from data. A rule engine checks every herb–drug pair against a curated, cited knowledge base: GREEN / AMBER / RED / NOT IN DATABASE, with timing advice.
  • Explain simply. AI rewrites only the matched evidence into plain Hindi or English, with sources and three questions to ask the doctor.
  • Report in one tap. A one-page PDF for the doctor and a pre-filled suspected-ADR report in the Ministry of Ayush format for the Ayush Suraksha portal.

Stack & data

Next.js + TypeScript PWA (Hindi/English UI) · FastAPI + Pydantic REST API · PostgreSQL in production, SQLite offline · Tesseract OCR · RapidFuzz matching (≥85 accept, 70–85 ask the user) · Groq LLM, JSON-schema output, temperature 0 · ReportLab PDFs. No model training; runs fully offline on a laptop.

40–60 high-frequency herb × drug pairs (15 common herbs × 12 drug classes) curated from PubMed, NIH LiverTox, MSKCC About Herbs, WHO monographs and the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India, graded on the Stockley's model. Decision support only: “ask your doctor”, never diagnosis or dosing.

4Expected impact

Patients & caregivers

A sourced answer before the first dose, in their own language, instead of silence or a guess. Free for citizens.

Doctors, vaidyas & chemists

Finally learn what patients actually take; a QR on the chemist's bill or an API inside hospital and telemedicine systems makes the check routine.

Ministry of Ayush

Structured, reproducible ADR reports and real co-use data from the catchment of its 99 peripheral pharmacovigilance centres.

Novelty

Nothing public today checks an Ayurvedic product against an allopathic prescription. TakeSure builds the missing bridge table: Indian brands × Ayurvedic herb synonyms × graded evidence, open and auditable. Honest by design: grades, timing gaps instead of alarms, “not in database” instead of guesses.
