The pitch — Shiwani speaks throughout

0:00 – 0:15 · THE STORY
Full frame, calm and direct. Raise the strip on "thyroid", the bottle on "ashwagandha".

"This is my mother's thyroid tablet. And this is the ashwagandha she takes for stress. Her doctor has never heard of the second one. Her chemist sold her both. And no one — no app, no label, no doctor — has ever checked whether they are safe together."

One-second pause after "together". Let it land before you move on.
0:15 – 0:30 · THE GAP, IN ONE NUMBER
Shiwani in the corner; slide 2 behind.

"She isn't unusual. Only 19 percent of Indian patients ever tell their doctor about herbal use, while more than a third take both systems together. India's pharmacovigilance programme for Ayurveda has received about 1,473 reports — in seven years. The two medical systems people already mix have no safety layer between them. I'm Shiwani from Team Bug Hunters, and TakeSure is that layer."

Zoom on 19% as you say it, then on 1,473.
0:30 – 1:05 · WHAT TAKESURE WOULD DO
Flowchart behind, highlight moving down as you speak. Tell it as your mother's story.

"Imagine my mother opens TakeSure. She photographs her prescription and the ashwagandha label. It reads the names, shows them back to her, and she confirms. Thyronorm becomes levothyroxine; the product becomes its herbs. Then a rule engine checks that pair against a curated knowledge base — every row with a severity, an evidence grade, and a published source. She sees Amber: ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone. Not an AI guess — a cited row. Only then does AI step in, to say it in simple Hindi and give her three questions for her next appointment. One tap, and a PDF goes to her doctor."

Pointer in sync: Scan → Match → Decide → Explain → Report. Say "Amber" slowly.
1:05 – 1:30 · THE TWIST
Novelty box, then the comparison table behind you.

"Here is what makes this different from every health chatbot: the verdict comes from published data; the AI only explains. Switch the AI off and the answer stays the same. And when we don't know, we say 'not in database' — we never guess. Underneath is a table nobody has built: Indian brand names, Ayurvedic herb names in Sanskrit, Hindi and Latin, and graded evidence — open and auditable. Medscape doesn't have it. Western herb databases don't know Himalaya or Dabur."

1:30 – 1:45 · WHO WINS
Slide 5 impact tiles behind.

"Patients get the answer before the first dose. Doctors finally learn what their patients take. And the Ministry of Ayush gets what it has never had — structured reaction reports and real data on what India is actually mixing."

1:45 – 1:55 · CLOSE
Back to full frame. Slow down. Look straight into the lens.

"We're not building another chatbot. We're building the missing safety layer between two medical systems. TakeSure. Lene se pehle, sure ho jao."

End card for 2 seconds: "TakeSure · Team Bug Hunters · Dhriti Garg · Shiwani Goyal · Khushi Gupta". No link, no QR code.

Delivery notes for a solo pitch

  • Record the six beats as six separate clips, two or three takes each. Cutting between clips hides any stumble and keeps energy up.
  • Vary the frame: full frame for the story and the close, corner frame over the slides in between. That alone makes a one-person video feel produced.
  • Look at the lens, not the screen. Put the script in a teleprompter app right under the camera.
  • Props carry the hook: the real strip and the real bottle, in frame for the whole first 15 seconds.
  • Two moments to slow down: the pause after "safe together" and the word "Amber".
  • Export the slides as PNGs, place them full-screen in Clipchamp or CapCut with your clip in the corner, turn on auto-captions, export 1080p MP4, upload to Drive with "Anyone with the link", paste on slide 1.
  • Rehearse three times to a timer. Target 1:50. Hard stop 2:00; if long, cut the Hindi line first.
