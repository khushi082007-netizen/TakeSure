The pitch

0:00 – 0:15 · THE STORY
Dhriti, full frame, calm and direct. Hold up the strip on "thyroid", the bottle on "ashwagandha".

"This is my mother's thyroid tablet. And this is the ashwagandha she takes for stress. Her endocrinologist has never heard of the second one. Her chemist sold her both. And no one — no app, no label, no doctor — has ever checked whether they are safe together."

Pause one second after "together". Let it land.
0:15 – 0:30 · THE GAP, IN ONE NUMBER
Dhriti, picture-in-picture over slide 2.

"She isn't unusual. Only 19 percent of Indian patients ever tell their doctor about herbal use, while more than a third take both systems together. India's pharmacovigilance programme for Ayurveda has received about 1,473 reports — in seven years. The two medical systems people already mix have no safety layer between them. We are Team Bug Hunters, and TakeSure is that layer."

Zoom on 19% as you say it, then on 1,473.
0:30 – 1:05 · WHAT TAKESURE WOULD DO
Shiwani, on camera for the first line, then over the flowchart with a highlight moving top to bottom. Tell it as the same mother's story.

"Imagine my mother opens TakeSure. She photographs her prescription and the ashwagandha label. It reads the names, shows them back to her, and she confirms. Thyronorm becomes levothyroxine; the product becomes its herbs. Then a rule engine checks that pair against a curated knowledge base — every row with a severity, an evidence grade, and a published source. She sees Amber: ashwagandha can raise thyroid hormone. Not from an AI guess — from a cited row. Only then does AI step in, to say it in simple Hindi, and give her three questions for her next appointment. One tap, and a PDF goes to her doctor."

Keep the pointer in sync: Scan → Match → Decide → Explain → Report.
1:05 – 1:30 · THE TWIST
Khushi, on camera for the first sentence, then over the novelty box and the comparison table.

"Here is what makes this different from every health chatbot: the verdict comes from published data; the AI only explains. Switch the AI off and the answer stays the same. And when we don't know, we say 'not in database' — we never guess. Underneath is the table nobody has built: Indian brand names, Ayurvedic herb names in Sanskrit, Hindi and Latin, and graded evidence — open and auditable. Medscape doesn't have it. Western herb databases don't know Himalaya or Dabur."

1:30 – 1:45 · WHO WINS
Khushi over slide 5.

"Patients get the answer before the first dose. Doctors finally learn what their patients take. And the Ministry of Ayush gets what it has never had — structured reaction reports and real data on what India is actually mixing."

1:45 – 1:58 · CLOSE
Cut to all three, standing together, looking into the camera.

Dhriti: "We're not building another chatbot."

Shiwani: "We're building the missing safety layer between two medical systems."

Khushi: "TakeSure." All: "Lene se pehle, sure ho jao."

End card: "TakeSure · Team Bug Hunters · CodeSlayer 2K26". Hold 2 seconds. No link, no QR code.

Delivery notes

  • This is a pitch, not narration: look at the camera, not at the slides. The slides appear only as backdrop behind the speaker.
  • Props carry the hook: the real strip and the real bottle. Keep them in frame for the whole first 15 seconds.
  • Three voices, one story. Each speaker picks up the same patient — "my mother", "she", "her doctor" — so it feels like one narrative.
  • One pause that matters: after "safe together" at 0:14. One word that matters: "Amber" at 0:55 — say it slowly.
  • Rehearse to a timer three times. Target 1:52. Hard stop 2:00.
